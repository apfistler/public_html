The Freedom of Forgiveness

Understanding, Processing, and Releasing Emotional Burdens

Author: Adam Fistler, BCH - Behavorial Change Consultant (adam@adamfistler.com) (adam@adamfistler.com)

What Is Forgiveness?

Forgiveness is one of the most misunderstood concepts in emotional healing.

Many people believe forgiveness means:

  • What happened was okay.
  • The other person was right.
  • You have to forget what happened.
  • You have to trust the person again.
  • You have to allow someone back into your life.
  • You have to stop caring about what happened.

That is not forgiveness.

Forgiveness is not about approving harmful behavior.

Forgiveness is not about denying your experience.

Forgiveness is about freeing yourself from carrying the emotional burden of what happened.

Forgiveness Is a Process of Understanding, Learning, and Releasing

When something painful happens, the mind naturally tries to process it.

It asks:

  • Why did this happen?
  • How could someone do this?
  • What does this mean?
  • How do I prevent this from happening again?
  • What should I have done differently?

Sometimes, when an experience is overwhelming, painful, or difficult to understand, the mind continues returning to it.

A grudge is often the mind's attempt to continue processing an experience that has not been fully resolved.

The mind keeps bringing it back because it believes:

"There is still something here I need to understand."

The Purpose of Processing

Before we can truly release something, we often need to understand it.

Forgiveness begins by looking honestly at what happened.

It means saying:

  • "I have looked at this situation."
  • "I acknowledge what happened."
  • "I acknowledge how it affected me."
  • "I understand, as much as I can, what happened and why it happened."
  • "I understand what this experience taught me."
  • "I have taken the lesson."
  • "Now I can release the burden."

The Difference Between the Memory and the Pain

A common misunderstanding is that forgiveness means forgetting.

It does not.

Your memories are part of your life experience.

The goal is not to erase the past.

The goal is to change your relationship with the past.

You can remember:

  • What happened.
  • What you learned.
  • The boundaries you need to maintain.

Without continuing to carry:

  • Anger.
  • Resentment.
  • Hurt.
  • Emotional pain.

Forgiveness keeps the wisdom.

It releases the weight.

The Hidden Cost of Holding Onto Anger

A grudge can feel like protection.

It may feel like:

  • "If I stay angry, I will remember what happened."
  • "If I stay angry, they cannot hurt me again."
  • "If I let go, they are getting away with it."

The mind is trying to protect you.

The problem is that the person who caused the pain may no longer be hurting you.

But the emotional wound remains active.

The event may have happened once.

The anger may cause you to relive it repeatedly.

The False Armor of a Grudge

Many people use anger as emotional armor.

The belief is:

"Holding onto this anger protects me."

But armor has a cost.

The same armor that protects you from being hurt again can also prevent you from experiencing peace.

A grudge keeps you connected to the person or event that caused the pain.

Forgiveness allows you to say:

  • "What happened mattered."
  • "It affected me."
  • "But it no longer controls me."

Forgiveness Does Not Mean What Happened Was Acceptable

Forgiveness and accountability are not opposites.

You can forgive someone and still recognize:

  • They were wrong.
  • Their behavior was harmful.
  • Their actions had consequences.
  • You need boundaries.

Forgiveness does not mean:

"I approve of what happened."

It means:

"I choose not to let what happened continue controlling my emotional life."

Forgive and Forget: A Misunderstood Phrase

The phrase:

"Forgive and forget"

often creates confusion.

Forgiveness does not require forgetting.

In fact, remembering can be valuable.

Your experiences teach you:

  • What boundaries you need.
  • What behaviors you will accept.
  • What patterns you want to avoid.
  • What kind of person you want to become.

A better phrase is:

  • Forgive and let go.
  • Let go of the emotional burden.
  • Keep the wisdom.

Why Some Anger Is Hard to Release

Sometimes anger feels bigger than the event itself.

This can happen because current experiences connect with unresolved emotional experiences from the past.

Emotional Resonance: The Piano and Guitar

Imagine a piano and a guitar.

Both instruments are tuned to the same note.

You press the A key on the piano.

The guitar begins to vibrate.

Why?

Because it resonates with the same frequency.

Our emotions can work similarly.

Throughout life, we experience events:

  • Being hurt.
  • Being rejected.
  • Feeling betrayed.
  • Feeling ignored.
  • Feeling powerless.

If those experiences are never fully processed, the emotional pattern can remain.

Later, a new event happens.

The current event activates the old emotional pattern.

The reaction feels bigger because it is not only about today.

It is also connected to yesterday.

Processing Old Emotional Patterns

To release old anger, we first need to acknowledge it.

A helpful process is:

Step 1: Identify the Person or Situation

  • Who or what still creates emotional pain?
  • Be specific.

Step 2: Identify What Happened

Write down:

"They hurt me when __________."

Then explore:

  • What happened?
  • What did I think about myself?
  • What did I think about them?
  • What did I feel?

Examples:

  • "They hurt me when they criticized me."
  • "That made me think I was not good enough."
  • "That made me feel ashamed and angry."

Step 3: Allow Yourself to Feel the Emotion

Many people try to skip this step.

They want to forgive before they have acknowledged the pain.

But emotions that are ignored often remain active.

Allow yourself to recognize:

  • The anger.
  • The sadness.
  • The hurt.
  • The disappointment.

The goal is not to stay there.

The goal is to process it.

Moving From Emotion to Understanding

After acknowledging the emotional experience, we can begin looking at it from a calmer perspective.

Ask:

  • What was this person thinking?
  • What needs were they trying to meet?
  • Were they intentionally trying to harm me?
  • Were they acting from their own pain, limitations, beliefs, or experiences?
  • Would they understand the impact of their actions?

Understanding does not excuse behavior.

Understanding allows healing.

Choosing Forgiveness

After processing the experience, you can decide:

"Am I ready to release this burden?"

Forgiveness is a choice.

You may say:

  • "I forgive you for __________."
  • "I forgive because __________."
  • "I release the anger and pain connected to this experience."
  • "I keep the lesson, and I release the burden."

Creating Emotional Freedom

Imagine there is a connection between you and the person who hurt you.

A connection made of:

  • Anger.
  • Hurt.
  • Resentment.
  • Unfinished emotions.

Imagine releasing that connection.

Not because the person was right.

Not because what happened did not matter.

But because you choose freedom.

After Forgiveness

Notice what changes.

Ask yourself:

  • What feels different now?
  • What emotions have become lighter?
  • What thoughts are different?
  • What choices become possible now?
  • What parts of my life can I improve now that I am no longer carrying this burden?

Forgiveness creates space.

Space for:

  • Peace.
  • Growth.
  • Healthier relationships.
  • New experiences.
  • A new way of seeing yourself.

Final Thoughts

Forgiveness is not weakness.

Forgiveness is not surrender.

Forgiveness is a process of strength.

It is the ability to look honestly at what happened, learn from the experience, and decide:

"I no longer need to carry this pain."

You cannot change the past.

But you can change how much power the past has over your present.

Understand the experience. Learn the lesson. Release the burden. Choose freedom.
